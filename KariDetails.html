Kari Recipe

Preparing the Kadhi Mixture

  1. Use 3/4 cup of gram flour (besan) and 1/2 kg of sour yogurt. If the yogurt isn't sour, leave it out of the fridge for 2–3 hours.
  2. Add 1/2 teaspoon of turmeric and some water.
  3. Blending: Blend these ingredients in a mixer jar for about 30 seconds to ensure a smooth texture without any lumps.
  4. Spice Paste: Separately blend 2 large or 3–4 small fresh green chilies, 5–6 thick garlic cloves, and a 1.5-inch piece of fresh ginger with a little water. This fresh paste gives the Kadhi its characteristic sharp flavor.
Preparing the Pakora Mix

  1. Vegetables: Finely chop 2 large onions, a few green chilies, and fresh coriander.
  2. Spices: Mix the vegetables with 1 teaspoon salt, 1 teaspoon red chili powder, 1 teaspoon crushed coriander seeds, 1 teaspoon cumin seeds, 1 teaspoon chili flakes, and a pinch (1/4 teaspoon) of carom seeds (ajwain).
  3. Tip: Mix the dry spices with the chopped vegetables first so they release their moisture and soften before adding the gram flour.
  4. Gram Flour: Add 1.5 cups of gram flour. Unlike regular crispy pakoras, these pakoras need a good amount of batter to ensure they stay soft inside the Kadhi. Do not add water immediately; keep the mixture dry until right before frying to prevent it from getting too watery.
Cooking the Kadhi Base

  1. Heat about 1/3 cup of cooking oil in a pan and saute the freshly prepared green chili-ginger-garlic paste.
  2. Add 2 finely chopped onions, some curry leaves, 2 teaspoons red chili powder, 1/2 teaspoon cumin powder, 1 tablespoon coriander powder, and 2 teaspoons salt. Saute the spices briefly on a low flame so they do not burn.
  3. Pour in the blended yogurt-besan mixture along with an additional 3 glasses of water.
  4. Tip: Do not just cook it quickly to thicken it. Let the mixture simmer over a low-to-medium flame for a long period so all the flavors deeply integrate.
Frying and Adding Pakoras

  1. Add a little water to the pakora mixture, mix it well, and deep-fry the pakoras until golden and crispy.
  2. Important Tip: Do not put hot pakoras directly into the Kadhi. Let them rest and cool for about 5 minutes so their internal moisture reduces. This prevents them from becoming mushy or breaking apart into a paste when added to the gravy.
Preparing the Tadka (Tempering)

  1. Use the leftover frying oil. First, cook chopped garlic until it starts turning light golden.
  2. Tip: Do not add whole dried red chilies right at the beginning with garlic, as they burn quickly before the garlic cooks. Add them along with cumin seeds once the garlic is slightly golden.
  3. Right before turning off the heat, add a pinch of chili powder for color and pour the hot tempering directly over the Kadhi. Garnish with fresh coriander.
